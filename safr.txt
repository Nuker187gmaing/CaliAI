1. MISSION STATEMENT
================================================================================
The Emergency Medical Services preserves life, promotes public safety, and
fosters economic growth through a commitment to prevention, preparedness,
response, and recovery as an all-risk life safety response provider.

EMS Vision: To provide great Emergency Medical Services by being metric driven,
technologically sophisticated, and community-focused while reflecting the people
we serve.

EMS CORE VALUES:
- Service      - Dedication to our Community
- Professionalism - Being professional within our community
- Integrity    - Upholding moral and ethical conduct at all times
- Respect      - Embracing diversity and recognizing individual worth
- Innovation   - Taking creative risks to adapt and improve
- Trust        - Reliance on the integrity, strength, and ability of our members

OPERATING PRINCIPLES:
1. Operate through Teamwork.
2. Operate to Position the Department for the Future.
3. Operate Ethically and with Integrity.

2. CHAIN OF COMMAND (COC)
================================================================================
Members of the EMS shall respect the chain of command at all times. In certain
instances an immediate supervisor may bypass COC in an emergency, but this must
not be abused. Failure to follow COC will result in punishment.

================================================================================
3. MISCONDUCT ACTION & CLOCK-IN PERMISSIONS
================================================================================
If an offense is committed, a Command member or Supervisor will reach out.
Strikes do NOT automatically expire from your record. The Chief has the final
say regarding punishment unless instructed otherwise.

STRIKE POLICY:
- Command members: no more than TWO strikes before being demoted one rank.
- High Command members: no more than ONE strike before being demoted one rank.
- If all ranks below are filled, the member may be assigned more than one rank
  lower than their previous rank.

SUSPENSION: If suspended, you are NOT allowed to go on-duty until your pending
report/punishment is resolved, regardless of whether the punishment is a
warning, strike, demotion, or termination.

CLOCK-IN PERMISSIONS:
- When clocking on-duty, be fully committed — do not clock in for 10-30 seconds
  just to revive a friend or anyone else while not actually on-duty.
- Clocking in solely to use revive permissions is Clock-in Abuse and may result
  in a BLACKLIST from the department.
- This is also a Global Bannable Offense.
- If you or another player was killed for no reason, call for staff instead of
  clocking on just to revive. The revive permission is for roleplay purposes
  only.

4. REINSTATEMENT POLICY
================================================================================
Those who have retired may talk with EMS Administration about being reinstated.
EMS Leadership follows section 7.2 (reserves/reinstatement) — read that section
before submitting resignation or reserves requests.

================================================================================
5. LEAVE OF ABSENCE (LOA)
================================================================================
- An excused Paramedic may take a LOA due to illness, real-life difficulties,
  computer problems, etc.
- Maximum LOA: 30 days (must request permission from EMS Administration).
- LOAs may NOT exceed 60 days total, or you will be encouraged to resign.
- Paramedics inactive for one month without LOA (after a 2-week activity
  warning) will be terminated but may be eligible for reinstatement.
- You may NOT go on LOA for server-related reasons (another department, staff,
  gangs, etc.).

6. RADIO COMMUNICATION POLICY
================================================================================
RTO should be quick and to the point at all times.

CALLING A MEMBER DIRECTLY:
  State their callsign, then yours: "1F-01 to 1F-03 direct"
  Wait for them to say "go ahead" before transmitting your message.

RESPONDING TO CALLS:    "[Callsign], Responding [postal]"
ARRIVING AT MEDICAL:    "[Callsign], at scene"
ARRIVING AT TC/FIRE:    "[Callsign], [fire callsign] at scene + brief description"

RTO NAME FORMAT: [Unit activated as] | [Name] | [Callsign]
Example: 1F-03 | ANGEL-1 | Huddy A.
Callsigns must NOT be altered.

7. UNIFORMS
================================================================================
The EMS has multiple uniforms designed for the vehicle being operated and the
call being responded to. Uniforms are found on the main EMS roster in
correspondence to rank or apparatus being operated.

================================================================================
8. DRIVING ETIQUETTE
================================================================================
Driving must stay within the law at all times unless Code 3.

RESPONSE CODES:
  Code 1 - Standard traffic laws. No violations permitted.
  Code 2 - No lights, no sirens, but emergent. Follow standard traffic laws
           with a sense of urgency. ALL STAGING CALLS ARE CODE-2.
  Code 3 - High priority, life-threatening. May break traffic regulations
           with restrictions:
             - EMS vehicle: never exceed 30 MPH above posted limit or 85 MPH.
             - Large apparatus (Engine, Brush, Ladder): never exceed 75 MPH.
             - All other vehicles (Sprint, Command, etc.): never exceed 100 MPH.
             - You MUST yield at all intersections even while Code 3.

9. STATIONING
================================================================================
The Emergency Medical Services has 9 Stations & Hospitals — one fire department
and hospital in each major area of the city.

================================================================================
10. CALL ALLOCATION
================================================================================
Unless there are multiple apparatuses of the same type, all units should respond
to the latest and/or highest priority EMS-related call (except Medevac, Firehawk,
or units told not to respond by the highest in RTO). If multiple units of the
same type are available, only one should be appointed unless disclosed otherwise
by the highest in RTO.

12. DISPATCH
================================================================================
When active dispatch is online, strict RTO immediately goes into effect:
- Do NOT self-dispatch to any calls; wait until dispatched.
- Do NOT ask permission to respond upon a call coming in — allow dispatch to
  get situated and dispatch the correct number of units.
- If dispatch does not dispatch anyone to a recent callout, you may then ask
  for permission to respond.
- While dispatch is active, your status MUST be marked and up to date in CAD.
  Failure to do so will result in punishment.

================================================================================
13. STAGING FOR 10-99s (OFFICER DOWN / EMS DOWN)
================================================================================
- 10-99 (Officer Down) calls must be handled with caution.
- You may ONLY move into a 10-99 scene if an officer enters your RTO and deems
  the scene safe.
- Otherwise, stage at least 5-6 blocks away from the scene.
- Exception: Major Operations Division - Critical Response Unit personnel.

================================================================================
14. INCIDENT COMMAND
================================================================================
- Captains and above are authorized to take Incident Command upon arriving on
  scene.
- The Incident Commander may allocate specific units to certain tasks. All units
  must answer to the scene's Incident Commander.
- If someone of a higher rank arrives (First Lieutenant or higher), they have
  authorization to take scene command.
- Scene command may also transfer if the scene requires someone from a specific
  subdivision (Major Operations Division, Fire & Rescue Division, Medevac &
  Coroner Division, etc.).

15. PARAMEDIC / EMT-B PROCEDURES
================================================================================

PATIENT TREATMENT PRIORITY:
- Treat the most critically injured patient first, regardless of occupation or
  criminal record. If a hurt officer and a critically injured suspect are on
  scene, treat the suspect first as they have a lower chance of survival.

RIGHT TO REFUSE TREATMENT:
- Patients have the right to refuse treatment and transportation.
- Inform them of the risks if they refuse.
- If the patient refuses without a guardian present, determine if they are in
  the right state of mind (ask: "What day is it?", "When is your birthday?",
  "What's your name?").

TRANSPORTING - PRIORITY LEVELS:
  Priority 1: Patient unstable/life-threatening. Use lights AND sirens.
  Priority 2: Transport urgent, patient stable but may worsen. Use lights;
              sirens only in heavy traffic.
  Priority 3: Minor injuries, no life-threatening conditions. Follow all
              traffic laws; no lights, no sirens.

NOTIFYING RTO OF A TRANSPORT:

  SECTION 1 (Only while dispatch is active):
  Ask if dispatch is ready: "Rescue-10 with priority [1/2/3] traffic when ready"
  Dispatch will say "Standby" or "Go-ahead." Once permitted, transmit:
    - Apparatus, priority of transport, patient description, chief complaint,
      and destination hospital.

  SECTION 2 (Required at ALL times during transport):
  Example: "Show Rescue-10 transporting 1 patient, priority 1, White Male,
  early 20s, chief complaint is going to be 1 GSW to the abdomen, to Sandy
  Shores Medical Center."

TRANSPORTING A SUSPECT:
- Suspect MUST be searched by a LEO before getting on the stretcher.
- Officers must accompany you back to the hospital (trailing or riding in back).

ARRIVING AT THE HOSPITAL:
- Remove patient from vehicle, take them in on the stretcher.
- Interact with stretcher and select "check patient in to hospital."
- Select the lowest time unless EMS Command instructs differently.
- LEO are NOT at liberty to instruct check-in time.
- If suspect is being hospitalized, find their bed and bring LEO so the suspect
  can be taken into law enforcement custody.

16. FIRE PROCEDURES
================================================================================

FIRE TYPES:
  Normal fire    - Extinguish with water or fire extinguisher.
  Chemical fire  - Green tint. Use foam hose (water INCREASES size) or
                   fire extinguisher.
  Electrical fire - "Lightning bolts" visible. Use foam hose (water INCREASES
                   size) or fire extinguisher.

ARRIVING ON SCENE:
- Put on "Turnout SCBA" gear before approaching any hazards.
- Perform a full 360-degree assessment of the fire.
- Determine fire type and whether to use foam or water.
- Keep all civilians and LEO away. They may not assist unless specifically
  asked by EMS.

USING AN ATTACK LINE (HOSE):
- Walk up to Engine, Ladder/Ladders, Brush-3841, LB-3387, or FR-10 and select
  "Pickup Fire Hose."
- Three hose settings (cycle with UP ARROW KEY):
    1. Water Gun
    2. Pressurized Water Gun
    3. Foam Gun (Brush-3841 & LB-3387 do NOT have foam)
- WARNING: This hose is strong enough to move a car. DO NOT SPRAY PEOPLE.

TANK DURATIONS (continuous spray until empty):
  ENGINE:              1 min 47 sec
  LADDER-10/LADDER-39: 2 min 8 sec
  BRUSH-3841:          2 min 40 sec  (NO FOAM)
  FR-10:               2 min 40 sec
  LB-3387:             2 min 40 sec  (NO FOAM)

TANK METER (bottom left of screen while holding hose):
  Green  = Full tank
  Yellow = Half full
  Red    = Empty or near empty

REFILLING / SUPPLY LINE:
- Park apparatus next to the nearest fire hydrant, turn lights on.
- Exit vehicle, walk up to hydrant, select "Pickup Fire Hose."
- Walk to apparatus and select "Attach Hydrant Hose."
- Tank refills in ~15 seconds.
- If attached to a hydrant while on a fire scene: INFINITE water.

10-50 INCIDENTS (MOTOR VEHICLE ACCIDENT):
- Block all incoming traffic from the accident upon arrival.
- Assess situation: how many individuals involved? Are patients trapped?
- Determine hazards via /me: "Checks to see if the vehicle is
  leaking/smoking WDIS?"
- If vehicle is an active fire threat, determine cause and extinguish with foam
  hose or fire extinguisher.
- For serious accidents, use /spreaders (Jaws of Life) to remove doors.
- NEVER remove an occupant from a vehicle without an EMT or Paramedic's
  permission.

17. GENERAL DEPARTMENT PROCEDURES
================================================================================
- All EMS personnel should open a department report on those who respawn on
  them during medical RP (this is FailRP). Evidence must be recorded and
  submitted.
- Trainees must NOT clock in before training. First offense: Strike.
  Second offense: Termination.
- Correct rank badge, uniform, and vehicles are MANDATORY at all times.

================================================================================
18. PASSIVE DEPARTMENT RULES
================================================================================
EMS is a passive department. This means:
- You should NOT have any weapons on your person.
- You should NOT be aggressive towards anyone.
- You should NOT shoot people with water.
- If attacked, call for Staff.
- EMS is NOT to participate, interfere, or otherwise get involved in LEO
  pursuits.
