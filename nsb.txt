NSB INTRODUCTION:
The National Security Bureau is a pre-main department offering 20+ elite custom
personal vehicles as you rank up, ranging from high-speed interceptors, heavy
armored tac vehicles, bulletproof patrol vehicles, to even Helicopters and Boats.
The NSB has 8 active subdivisions with directors looking to fill the ranks up and
get RP scenes going (minimum Patrol Agent 2 required for subdivisions).
NSB also offers "gang tags" known as supervisor or Command team tags for
representing Rank or Subdivision (e.g., TRT / Recruitment Team tag).

NSB OVERVIEW & PURPOSE:
Focus Areas:
- Federal Intelligence (Neutralization of Criminal Networks)
- Operational Protocol (Rules of Engagement & Tactical Movement)
- High-Risk Interdiction (Vehicle Pursuit & Interception of Prohibited Weapons)

The National Security Bureau operates as federal authority, tasked with the
preservation of national stability and the suppression of high-level criminal
enterprises. Unlike local agencies, the NSB focuses exclusively on Federal
Offenses, including the trafficking of illegal firearms, the manufacture of
prohibited explosives, and the apprehension of violent federal fugitives.
NSB does NOT focus on minor traffic safety. Focus is on the suppression of
illegal arms, explosives, and federal fugitives.

PROFESSIONAL CONDUCT & FEDERAL PRESENCE:

Commanding Presence:
- NSB personnel do not engage in trash talk or heated arguments with civilians.
- Authority is derived from your badge and your composure.
- If a civilian is being difficult, remain clinical and objective.

Controlled Speech:
- Use clear, professional sentences.
- Instead of "Hey, get out of the car right now!", say "Driver, exit the vehicle
  and face away from my voice. Do it now!"

Information Gathering:
- Every interaction is an opportunity for intelligence.
- Even during a release (if no felony is found), note the individual's
  associations, vehicle contents, and destination.

Professional Authority:
- Treat local officers with respect, but maintain the boundary when federal
  investigations take priority.
- If a local officer is handling a minor 10-11, do not interfere unless it
  escalates to a felony or you are told to do so.
- Do NOT discuss ongoing Bureau Investigations on open radio frequencies or in
  public areas like Legion Square or within voice range of civilians.

Operational Principles:
- Request LEO units for misdemeanor arrests involving traffic violations or
  arrests that don't involve federal agents.
- Never overtake scene command / callouts from someone unless absolutely
  necessary.
- Always follow code 1-3 responses to calls.

MEDICAL ROLEPLAY & COORDINATION:
When a scene turns cold or a suspect is downed, NSB agents must transition to
professional medical RP.
- The Check: Start with /me checks pulse and breathing.
- The Follow-up: State "dead" or "Pulse 50".
- The Call: Once status is confirmed, notify Dispatch. If stable but injured,
  request EMS/Hospital transport. If no pulse and RP dictates fatality, request
  Coroner's Office for body processing.
- The Goal: Never respond to a scene and leave while there are multiple dead
  bodies. Always clear scenes with correct medical RP.

TECHNICAL PROTOCOLS - DUTY & IDENTIFICATION:
Clocking In: Your shift officially begins the moment you go /duty. This tracks
your hours and syncs your permissions. You must be in full uniform with assigned
gear ready before hitting the streets.

Plate Formatting: All NSB personal vehicles and patrol units MUST follow the
official plate format:
xxNSBxxx (Example: 02NSB011) — IT CANNOT BE ANYTHING OTHER THAN THIS FORMAT
- The first two numbers (xx) are the last 2 digits of your Callsign.
- NSB identifies the Department.
- The last three numbers (xxx) are your full callsign number.
- Example: Callsign NSB-125 → plate is 25NSB125

RADIO COMMUNICATIONS & RTO:
RTO (Radio Traffic Only): Official dispatch frequency. Broken into 3
jurisdictions: Sandy / LA / Paleto. Treat with 100% professionalism. No yelling,
no background noise, no unnecessary chatter.

Golden Rules of the Radio:
- Don't Interrupt: Never speak over another agent.
- The 3-Second Rule: Wait 3-5 seconds after a transmission ends before speaking.
- Emergency Override: Only use "Break, Break, Break" to interrupt a transmission
  for life-threatening emergencies (10-99 only).
- Signal 100: Radio is restricted — only transmit info directly related to the
  active emergency.
- Always use Callsign | Officer Name format in RTO. Example: NSB-10 | T. Smith

RTO Callout Format: CALLSIGN / 10-code / Postal / Description / 32s
Example: "NSB-10, I got a 10-11 at 432 southbound, on a 4-door Durango,
requesting one additional."
Never call out what you are pulling over — always say your postal and situation
BEFORE the suspect/vehicle description.

Priority 10-Codes for NSB:
- 10-11 (Traffic Stop): Use for every interdiction. Give 10-20 (location),
  vehicle description, and occupant count.
- 10-80 (Vehicle Pursuit): Initiate immediately when suspect flees. Keep
  transmissions short: direction, speed, street names.
- 10-99 (Officer in Distress): Highest priority. Use ONLY if your life is in
  immediate danger. All units must drop everything and respond.
- Code 5 (Felony Stop): High-risk tactics required. Guns drawn, backup mandatory.

USE OF FORCE CONTINUUM:
The Use of Force is a strict ladder. Only move to the next step if the current
one is not working. Never escalate without cause.
1. Officer Presence: Being there in uniform. Often stops a crime before it starts.
2. Verbal Commands: Calm, firm, non-threatening instructions to comply.
3. Physical Control: Hand-to-hand restraining. Using bare hands to subdue a
   non-cooperative suspect.
4. Impact Weapons: Deploy the Baton if a suspect becomes physically aggressive.
5. Less-Lethal: Use the Taser when physical force is not enough but lethal force
   is not yet justified.
6. Lethal Force: Use Firearms to stop a deadly threat.

Lethal Force Rules:
- Authorization: If lower rank, always ask before using lethal force when other
  units are nearby — UNLESS you are in immediate life-threatening danger.
- The Only Way Out Rule: Lethal force is a last resort to prevent severe injury
  or death to yourself or others.
- Consequences: Unjustified shooting results in an Internal Affairs investigation
  and can lead to immediate termination.

RESPONSE CODES:
Code 1 (Normal): No lights/sirens. Follow all traffic laws.
Code 2 (Urgent): Lights on, sirens only for intersections. Priority response for
non-life-threatening calls.
Code 3 (Emergency): Lights and sirens active. Rapid response for life-threatening
calls. Can bypass traffic laws but must maintain public safety first.
Code 4 (Stable): Scene is under control. Backup can cancel or move in slowly.
Code 5 (High-Risk): Felony Stop. Used for stolen cars, felony warrants, or
dangerous BOLOs.
Code 6 (Searching): On-scene or in the immediate area investigating.

DRIVING RULES:
- NEVER PIT a vehicle going over 130 MPH. Only PIT at 130 MPH and under.
- Limit speed during harsh weather conditions to about 130 MPH.
- Always drive realistically. Example: driving a Lamborghini up a mountain is
  NOT permitted.

MIRANDA RIGHTS:
Miranda rights are the basic rights of any suspect being placed under arrest.
You MUST read the suspect their rights before questioning — otherwise all their
testimony will be invalidated. You do NOT have to read rights if the suspect is
not being questioned.

Miranda Rights Script:
"You have a right to remain silent. Anything you say or do, may and will be used
against you in the court of law. You have a right to an attorney. If you cannot
afford one, one will be provided to you by the state of California. Do you
understand these rights that I just read to you, and with those rights in mind,
do you wish to speak to me any further?"
